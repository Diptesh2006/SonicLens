# Blinded sound value check

This protocol is a small exploratory check of whether the sonification communicates a preselected pattern. It is not an accessibility evaluation and cannot establish that sound improves analysis, speed, accuracy, or user outcomes.

## Materials

- Use a deterministic synthetic dataset with a known event recorded before the listening session.
- Prepare 12 short clips in randomized order: six contain a seeded abrupt increase and six contain a matched no-event segment. Keep duration and loudness settings consistent. Use the same documented mapping and do not speak labels over the clips.
- Have someone who does not administer the session assign opaque clip IDs and retain the answer key until responses are recorded.

## Session

1. Explain headphones/volume controls and that participation is optional. Do not train participants on which clip should contain an event.
2. Play each clip once in randomized order. Ask the listener to record “event”, “no event”, or “unsure” and confidence from 1–5.
3. Optionally show the chart after all responses and ask which evidence would help verify the judgment. Record observations without prompting toward a positive response.
4. Reveal the answer key only after all responses are locked. Report counts, misses, false alarms, unsure responses, and confidence descriptively, alongside participant count and any protocol deviations.

## Interpretation

Treat results as formative product feedback only. Do not generalize from a small sample, claim improved accessibility or performance, or present successful detection as evidence that the sound is useful for other datasets or users. Preserve the clips, randomization seed, answer key, anonymized responses and analysis notes so the exercise can be repeated. Obtain appropriate consent and review before collecting or retaining participant data.

No participant study or sound-value result is claimed by this document; it defines a repeatable protocol for a future evaluation.
